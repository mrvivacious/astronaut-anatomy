What NASA pays astronauts, and what the path to qualifying costs. Each figure links to its source. Costs are published averages (sticker prices), not what any one person will pay: grants, scholarships, and military service change the math.

Figures last checked November 1, 2026.


img source: futurism.com

salary

  • $152,258 per year. NASA says this reflects the 2024 pay schedules and is an approximation.

    Source: NASA, Becoming an Astronaut FAQ (page last updated Aug 5, 2025)

  • Military astronaut candidates are detailed to Johnson Space Center and stay on active-duty status for pay, benefits, and leave.

    Source: NASA, Becoming an Astronaut FAQ

  • In space: NASA told Fortune that astronauts aboard the space station get their regular salary, with transportation, lodging, and meals provided. Fortune reports an extra allowance of about $5 a day (about $1,430 for Suni Williams and Butch Wilmore's 286-day mission) and no overtime or hazard pay.

    Source: Fortune, Sept 24, 2025

what NASA's requirements cost (published averages, before aid)

  • U.S. citizenship. Required. Applicants with valid U.S. dual citizenship are also eligible.

    Source: NASA, Becoming an Astronaut FAQ

  • Bachelor's degree, the usual first step toward NASA's master's requirement. Average published prices for 2025-26:

    Public four-year, in-state: $11,950 a year in tuition and fees, about $47,800 over four years. The average full budget (tuition, housing, food, books, transportation, other expenses) is $30,990 a year, about $123,960 over four years.

    Private nonprofit four-year: $45,000 a year in tuition and fees, about $180,000 over four years. Full budget: $65,470 a year, about $261,880 over four years.

    These are sticker prices. After grant aid, College Board estimates first-time, full-time students pay about $2,300 a year in tuition and fees at public four-year colleges (in-state) and about $16,910 at private nonprofit colleges. Source: College Board, Trends in College Pricing and Student Aid 2025

  • STEM master's degree. Required. The average tuition and required fees for full-time graduate students at public institutions is $12,116 a year (2024-25), so about $24,200 for a two-year program, tuition and fees only.

    This is an average of what institutions charge, across graduate programs, not what students actually pay, and it excludes living costs. NASA also accepts two years of doctoral work in a STEM field, a medical degree (MD or DO), or completion of test pilot school in place of the master's; those are not priced here. Sources: NCES IPEDS Trend Generator, NASA, Become an Astronaut (updated Oct 30, 2026)

  • Experience. NASA requires 3 years of related professional experience after your degree, or, for pilots, 1,000 hours of pilot-in-command time with at least 850 hours in high-performance jet aircraft. Flying experience is not required. The first option is paid employment. NASA says jet experience is usually obtained through the military.

    Sources: NASA, Become an Astronaut, NASA, Becoming an Astronaut FAQ

  • NASA long-duration flight astronaut physical. Required. NASA does not grant waivers for its medical requirements. No cost figure is listed here because none was found in NASA's published materials.

    Sources: NASA, Becoming an Astronaut FAQ, NASA medical standard (PDF)

  • Example total, public-school path, tuition and fees only: 4 × $11,950 + 2 × $12,116 is about $72,000, before aid and not counting living costs.
